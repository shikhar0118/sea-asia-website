CUSTOMS HOUSE AGENCY

Simplifying customs clearance. Connecting global trade.

A Customs House Agent (CHA), now commonly referred to under Indian regulations as a Customs Broker, acts as an authorised intermediary between importers, exporters, and customs authorities.

A Customs Broker assists businesses with the documentation, regulatory formalities, coordination, and procedures required for importing and exporting goods through seaports, airports, and land customs stations. The primary objective is to ensure that cargo clearance procedures are handled accurately, efficiently, and in accordance with applicable customs regulations.

For businesses involved in international trade, professional customs clearance support helps simplify complex procedures, minimise documentation errors, and facilitate the smooth movement of cargo.

Terminology note: Customs Brokers are governed by the Customs Brokers Licensing Regulations, 2018, under the Customs Act, 1962.

THE CLEARANCE JOURNEY

How the customs clearance process works

The actual process and documentation may vary depending on cargo type, customs station, applicable regulations, and examination requirements.

  1. 01

    Document collection

    Collect commercial invoices, packing lists, transport documents, IEC details, and other applicable documents.

  2. 02

    Documentation & filing

    Prepare and file the applicable Bill of Entry for imports or Shipping Bill for exports.

  3. 03

    Customs assessment

    Coordinate with customs authorities for assessment, classification, valuation, and applicable duty formalities.

  4. 04

    Cargo examination

    Coordinate physical examination or inspection whenever required by customs authorities.

  5. 05

    Customs clearance approval

    Follow up on the required customs clearance orders, including Out of Charge for imports or Let Export Order for exports.

  6. 06

    Cargo release & movement

    Coordinate cargo release and onward transportation arrangements.

PREPARE YOUR SHIPMENT

Documents required for customs clearance

An indicative list to help you prepare before contacting our team. Requirements depend on the goods, origin or destination, and applicable regulations.

Import customs clearance

  • Commercial Invoice: details of goods, quantity, value, seller, and buyer.
  • Packing List: details of packages, weights, dimensions, and contents.
  • Bill of Lading / Airway Bill: transport document for the imported shipment.
  • IEC and GST details: Importer Exporter Code and applicable registration details.
  • Other applicable documents, such as Certificate of Origin, insurance documents, import permits or licences, and product-specific certificates or declarations.

Export customs clearance

  • Commercial Invoice.
  • Packing List.
  • Shipping Instructions.
  • Shipping Bill.
  • Export licences or permissions, wherever applicable.
  • Certificate of Origin, where required.
  • Other product-specific supporting documents.

Please note: This is an indicative list, not a universal checklist. Requirements depend on the nature of goods, country of origin or destination, and applicable customs regulations.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Customs clearance FAQs

Answers to common questions from importers and exporters.

What is a Customs House Agent (CHA)?

A Customs House Agent (CHA), commonly known today as a Customs Broker, is an authorised professional who assists importers and exporters with customs clearance formalities, documentation, and coordination with customs authorities.

What services does a CHA provide?

A CHA provides assistance with import and export customs clearance, Bill of Entry filing, Shipping Bill filing, document verification, customs examination coordination, assessment follow-ups, and cargo release procedures.

What is the difference between a Bill of Entry and a Shipping Bill?

A Bill of Entry is the primary customs declaration used for imported goods, while a Shipping Bill is the principal customs declaration used for goods exported by sea or air.

Why do importers and exporters need CHA services?

CHA services help businesses navigate customs procedures, manage documentation, coordinate with relevant authorities, and address procedural requirements during cargo clearance.

What documents are required for import customs clearance?

Common documents include a commercial invoice, packing list, Bill of Lading or Airway Bill, IEC details, and other applicable certificates or permits.

What is customs examination?

Customs examination is the process in which customs authorities inspect goods, physically or through other prescribed procedures, to verify shipment details and regulatory compliance.

What is a Shipping Bill?

A Shipping Bill is a customs declaration filed for export goods, containing details such as the exporter, goods description, quantity, value, and other required information.

What is a Bill of Entry?

A Bill of Entry is a customs declaration filed by an importer or authorised representative for imported goods, providing relevant shipment and goods information.

What is the meaning of Out of Charge (OOC)?

Out of Charge is the customs clearance order that permits imported goods to be cleared for home consumption after the applicable requirements have been fulfilled.

What is Let Export Order (LEO)?

Let Export Order is the customs clearance permission granted for export goods after the applicable export formalities have been completed.

Can a CHA help resolve customs documentation errors?

Yes. A Customs Broker can assist in identifying documentation discrepancies, coordinating corrections, and responding to relevant procedural queries.
