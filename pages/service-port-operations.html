PORT OPERATIONS

Common port and cargo handling terms

Processes may vary by cargo, terminal, and location.

Facilities & movement

  • Port terminal: facility where cargo and containers are received, handled, stored, loaded, or unloaded.
  • CFS: Container Freight Station for receiving, consolidating, deconsolidating, and handling cargo.
  • ICD: Inland Container Depot providing inland container handling and related logistics services.
  • Gate-in / Gate-out: entry into or authorized exit from a designated facility.
  • Container movement: transport between ports, terminals, CFS facilities, warehouses, and other locations.

Handling & release

  • Cargo examination: inspection by Customs or another authorized agency, where applicable.
  • Cargo cut-off: deadline for cargo or containers to reach a facility for a vessel.
  • Delivery Order (DO): document issued by a shipping line or agent to facilitate cargo release.
  • Cargo handover: transfer to a shipping line, terminal, transporter, or authorized recipient.

Please note: Port, terminal, customs, and cargo handling requirements depend on the shipment and applicable procedures. Timelines and approvals are controlled by the relevant authorities and service providers.

COMMON QUESTIONS

Port and cargo handling FAQs

Answers to common questions about port operations.

What is port and cargo handling coordination?

It involves coordinating cargo movement, terminal entry, handling requirements, examination arrangements, release, and dispatch at designated port facilities.

What does a port terminal do?

A port terminal is a facility where cargo and containers are received, handled, stored, inspected, loaded, unloaded, or transferred for onward transportation.

What is the difference between CFS and ICD?

A CFS is generally near a seaport and supports cargo and container handling. An ICD is an inland facility that provides container handling and related logistics services.

What is container gate-in?

Gate-in is the entry of a container or cargo vehicle into a designated terminal or facility after completing applicable requirements.

What is cargo examination?

It is an inspection by Customs or another authorized agency to verify shipment details, goods, quantities, or applicable requirements.

Do you coordinate container movement and transportation?

Yes. We coordinate with transporters and logistics partners for movement between designated ports, terminals, CFS facilities, warehouses, and other locations.

What is a cargo cut-off time?

It is the deadline for cargo or containers to reach a designated facility for a vessel. Missing it may affect the planned schedule.

Do you assist with cargo release and delivery?

Yes. We coordinate applicable release documents, delivery orders, terminal requirements, transportation, and dispatch arrangements.

Can port operations be delayed?

Yes. Congestion, weather, documentation, customs examination, equipment availability, and other operational conditions can affect timelines.

Do you coordinate import and export cargo?

Yes. We provide port and cargo handling coordination for import and export shipments, subject to applicable requirements.

What documents are needed for port cargo entry?

Requirements may include shipping documents, container details, gate passes, delivery orders, customs documents, and terminal-specific paperwork.

How do you support smooth cargo movement?

We coordinate documentation, operational deadlines, communication with relevant stakeholders, and follow-up on release and delivery requirements.
